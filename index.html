How our debates work

01
The room votes first
Everyone votes on the motion before anyone speaks. It is a private ballot by QR code.
02
Both sides make the case
Openings, two rounds of open forum, then closings. The floor is open to any member and there is no sign-up to speak.
03
The room votes again
The side that changes more minds wins, even if it finishes in the minority.

The side that wins is the one that changed the most minds, not the one that started ahead. Every result in the archive is scored that way.

About the National Civic Society

A debating society for American high schools

The National Civic Society (N.C.S.) brings free speech, open argument, and critical thinking into American high schools. At every meeting, members take a contested question in politics, ethics, or current affairs and debate it from both sides, examining it below the surface and testing their own views against people who see it differently.

N.C.S. grew out of a society founded at Seattle Academy in 2025. A small group of students who were drawn to large, difficult questions found no place in school to discuss them across different friend groups and backgrounds, so they started one.

Once the society was established, its founders created the National Civic Society to bring it to other schools. Each chapter is student-led and follows the same standing orders, and any high school in the country can start one.

Debates are held at every meeting, with the floor open to any member and no sign-up required to speak. N.C.S. takes no position of its own; members argue every side of a question, and the room votes before and after, with the result going to whichever side changes more minds. Some members come to sharpen how they argue, others to get more comfortable speaking in front of a room, and many simply to spend an hour in a genuine conversation with people they disagree with.

See the Schedule for what is coming up, or the Archive for how past debates were decided.

Joining N.C.S. gives you a place to argue hard questions with people who see them differently, and to work through them in the open. We would be glad to have you.

Standing orders · rev. 1

Rules of procedure

1 · The gavel

|
One strike
The speaker should finish their current sentence and conclude.
||
Two strikes
The speaker's time has ended and they should stop.

The chair signals with the gavel rather than interrupting verbally. The chair explains the gavel during the opening at the first meeting of the year.

2 · Order of proceedings

2.1
The timer is displayed to the whole room
The countdown runs on the room's screen, visible to every member.
2.2
Each side gives an opening before open forum begins
The proposition speaks first, then the opposition, each without interruption. Open forum follows.
2.3
A member may not take two consecutive turns in open forum
At least one other person must speak between any member's turns. The chair may call on a speaker who has not yet contributed.
2.4
Floor contributions are statements, not questions
A member using floor time makes an argument rather than asking a question. Each contribution is limited to 45 seconds.
2.5
Speakers are not required to respond to any given floor contribution
Speakers choose which floor points to address. What is raised and what is answered is recorded in the log.
2.6
The proposition delivers the final closing
Closings run opposition first, then proposition.
2.7
The chair marks the transition between open-forum blocks
Between the two open-forum blocks the chair restates the motion and summarizes where the disagreement stands. No vote is taken.
2.8
Floor rounds absorb changes to timing
The chair may add a fourth floor contribution when ahead of schedule, or reduce a round to two when behind. A floor round is not cut in favor of a main speaker.
2.9
Two floor speakers are arranged in advance
Before each debate the chair arranges for two members to speak first when the floor opens.

3 · Voting

3.1 Method
Private ballot by QR codeMembers vote through a form linked by a QR code on the room's screen.
3.2 Timing
Once before, once afterThe room votes at the opening and at the close. There is no midpoint vote.
3.3 Result
Decided by the larger change in the voteThe side that shifts more of the room between the two votes wins, regardless of the final majority. The result is announced and recorded in the archive.

4 · Speaker selection

4.1 Qualifying
Drawn from the floor logSpeaking slots are offered to members whose floor contributions have been recorded in the log.
4.2 Reserved slot
One slot per debate for a first-time speakerOf the four speaking positions, one is held for a member who has not previously spoken.
4.3 Rotation
A speaker sits out the next two debatesAfter speaking, a member is not eligible for a speaking slot for the next two debates.
4.4 Confirmation
A one-line thesis, 48 hours beforeSelected speakers submit one sentence stating their position two days before the debate. A speaker who does not confirm is replaced by the reserve.
4.5 Non-attendance
Two additional debates before eligibilityA speaker who fails to appear without notice sits out two debates beyond the standard rotation.
